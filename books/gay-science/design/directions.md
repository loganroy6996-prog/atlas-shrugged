# The Gay Science — Three Identity Directions

Book: *The Gay Science* (Friedrich Nietzsche, 1882/1887), 448 chapters, 8 parts.
Designer: SupGayScience-2.GsIdentityDesigner. Baseline inspected 2026-10-15
(cover + Preface + §125 at 1440×1000, served from repo root).

## What the book actually is (design evidence)

- The title is the Provençal troubadours' *gaya scienza* — "joyful science",
  the art of poetry; Nietzsche glosses it as the unity of **singer, knight,
  and free spirit** (Preface, "Over my front door").
- Structure is a song-cycle: Preface (1 long essay) → **Prelude in rhymes**
  (63 tiny poems: "For Dancers", "Stellar Morals", "Invitation") → four books
  of aphorisms Books 1–4 (§1–342) → Book 5 "We Fearless Ones" (41 long essays)
  → **Appendix of Songs** (Prince Vogelfrei).
- Obsessive images, sampled from real chapter text: the **sun at noon**
  (Incipit tragoedia: Zarathustra steps before the sun; "Get on the ships!"
  wishes "many such new suns"), the **sea and ships** (§289, §291 Genoa,
  §310 Will and wave), **dancing** ("the kingdom of the dance", Epilogue;
  "For Dancers": "slipp'ry ice is paradise"), **stars** ("Stellar Egotism",
  "Stellar Morals", §279 Star friendship), the **madman's lantern** at bright
  morning (§125), **masks/theatre/song** (Book 2 on art, Appendix songs).
- Tone arc: mischievous rhymes → aphoristic skirmishes → the death of God at
  the center (Book 3, §125) → amor fati sunrise (Book 4 "St Januarius",
  January saint of new beginnings) → fearless open-sea essays → outlaw songs.
  Any identity must span **playfulness and abyss** without splitting in two.

## References studied (with citations)

1. **Munch, *The Sun* (1909–11, Univ. of Oslo aula mural)** — viewed full
   painting via Wikimedia Commons (Google Art Project scan). Takeaway: a
   modernist sun is not a disc with rays but a *radiating architecture* —
   long wedge rays that organize the whole wall, cliffs/sea as flanking
   masses, high-key color on a vast field. Directly relevant: Munch painted
   it under Nietzsche's influence ("Between us and the Sun", Munchmuseet).
   (edvardmunch.org/the-sun.jsp; commons File:Edvard Munch - The Sun -
   Google Art Project.jpg)
2. **Matisse, *La Danse* (1910)** — studied via Artsper analysis + Wikipedia.
   Takeaway: five vermilion bodies + green ground + blue sky = maximum joy
   from three flat colors; the dance *ring* as the compositional unit; figures
   reduced to gesture. Grammar source for a ring-of-dancers motif and for a
   strict 3-color discipline. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_(Matisse);
   blog.artsper.com "Art Analysis: Dance by Henri Matisse")
3. **Tarot de Marseille, *Le Soleil* (XIX)** — studied via Marselia deck
   comparison + tarot-heritage.com iconography essay. Takeaway: the Provençal
   sun-face with straight/wavy alternating rays, twin figures under a wall —
   a southern-French vernacular sun with a face, naïve geometry, red/yellow/
   blue primaries. Authentic to the book's Occitan roots (1323 Consistori del
   Gay Saber, Toulouse). (marselia.app/tarot-decks/compare/le-soleil;
   tarot-heritage.com/from-trionfi-to-majorarcana/il-sole-le-soleil-the-sun/)
4. **Consistori del Gay Saber / *Leys d'amor* illuminated initial** (Toulouse,
   14th c.) — studied via encyclopedia summaries. Takeaway: the literal
   historical "Gay Science" had an illuminated-manuscript visual culture:
   historiated initials, gold leaf, rubric red, vine borders. (Consistori del
   Gay Saber summaries; drac.cultura.gencat.cat troubadour catalogue PDF)
5. **Genoa / Mediterranean modernism** — from the book itself (§291 Genoa,
   "Get on the ships!"): sun-bleached stone, deep-sea blue, ship prows,
   noon glare. Cross-checked against Mediterranean-poster palettes (flat sea
   bands, cream sails, black rigging lines).

## Direction A — "NOON" (Mediterranean modernist mural)

**One-line:** A Munch/Matisse sun-mural: the book as one long day climbing
toward noon and sailing past it.

- **Palette (dark-first):** Abyssal Sea `#06222B` (ground), Noon Gold
  `#F5B301` (sun), Dance Vermilion `#E4572E` (figures/accents), Wave
  Ultramarine `#1D4ED8`-deepened `#173A8F` (sea bands), Foam Bone `#F3EAD8`
  (text), Dusk Aubergine `#2A1540` (night chapters).
- **Type:** Fraunces (Google Fonts, variable, SOFT/WONK axes) for display —
  joyful, slightly drunk letterforms that can shout or sing; Source Serif 4
  (Google Fonts) for body; both load via the existing Google-Fonts link
  pattern (supervisor adds families to index.html). Scale: cover title
  clamp(3.5rem, 12vw, 7rem); chapter titles clamp(2–3.5rem) Fraunces 600 with
  tight leading; body 1.3rem/1.85.
- **Layout:** cover = full-bleed mural: huge rising sun-disc low-center,
  wedge rays organizing the wall, title set *across* the sun like a mural
  inscription; chapter openers = sun-disc *position* grammar (see below),
  text measure calm and centered-left.
- **Illustration medium:** original SVG mural-work: flat radiating wedges,
  concentric sea bands, a ring-of-dancers silhouette drawn as bold bezier
  gestures (Matisse-grammar, Nietzsche-subject: the dance ring from the
  Epilogue's "kingdom of the dance").
- **Signature idea:** the **day-cycle sun**. Part I: sun below horizon (glow
  only). Prelude: sunrise sparkle. Books 1–2: climbing. Book 3 (§125, the
  madman at bright morning): sun at zenith, harshest light, lantern motif
  inverted (lantern lit *at noon*). Book 4 (St Januarius): golden applause
  of rays. Book 5: long fearless afternoon shadows. Appendix: sun-set red
  disc over a black sea, outlaw-ship silhouette. The reader *feels* the day
  pass; the sun's altitude is the book's progress bar.
- **Navigation:** TOC parts as horizon-lines with the sun at that part's
  altitude; progress fill = a ray sweeping gold→vermilion.
- **Chapter-variation grammar:** altitude (part) × weather (cohort):
  short aphorisms get a small authored marginal sun-spark/ray-tick sized to
  the text; landmark chapters (§125 madman, §341 heaviest weight, §342
  Incipit tragoedia, §343, §382–383, Appendix songs) get full-bleed ray
  openers; Prelude rhymes get dance-ring fragments that complete the ring
  across the 63 poems.
- **Why THIS book:** no other book in the library is a sun-worship liturgy
  that opens with dance rhymes and ends with a man stepping before the sun.
  Swap the title to "Beyond Good and Evil" and a dancing noon-sun mural is
  nonsensical — that book is midnight oil, not noon glare.

## Direction B — "CHANSONNIER" (troubadour illuminated manuscript)

**One-line:** The book as its own ancestor: a 14th-century Occitan songbook
(*chansonnier*) rebound for the screen — gold leaf, rubrics, vine borders.

- **Palette (dark-first):** Lampblack Vellum `#14100B` (ground), Gold Leaf
  `#D9A441`, Rubric Minium `#C33B2A` (versals, first-lines), Lapis Night
  `#1F3A93` (historiated panels), Parchment `#EDE0C3` (text), Vine Green
  `#5A7247` (border acanthus).
- **Type:** IM Fell English (Google Fonts) for display + drop versals —
  genuine 17th-c. punchcut voice, the closest digital type to early print;
  Cormorant Garamond (already loaded in index.html) kept for body to avoid
  a second download. Scale deliberately *bookish*: modest titles, enormous
  historiated initials (5–7 lines), generous margins for vine borders.
- **Layout:** cover = a full-page miniature in a tooled gold frame:
  roundel of the singer-knight-free-spirit (lyre/sword/ship) ringed by the
  title set as a colophon; chapters open like manuscript leaves: rubric
  first-line in minium, historiated initial, vine running down the left
  margin whose leaves multiply with chapter length.
- **Illustration medium:** original SVG "illumination": tooled-gold
  roundels (concentric punched-dot rings), knotwork dividers, eight
  historiated part-miniatures (lantern, lyre, mask, ship, sun-face, star,
  wave, dancing fool) in flat lapis/minium/gold.
- **Signature idea:** **rubrication + historiated initials.** Every chapter
  gets a real illuminated initial drawn for its opening letter-family and a
  rubricated first line; the vine-border's growth encodes length (a 12-word
  aphorism gets one leaf; the Preface gets a full hunt-scene border). The
  Appendix becomes a songbook gathering with staff-line rules.

- **Navigation:** TOC as a calendar/quire table: parts as gatherings
- **Chapter-variation grammar:** initial-size × border-growth × roundel:
  content picks the miniature (e.g. §125 madman = lantern roundel; §310 =
  wave roundel; dance poems = fool roundel); length picks vine growth.
  448 chapters stay cheap: 8 roundels + ~12 initial families + vine
  segments recombined, zero per-chapter files.
- **Why THIS book:** the title *is* the troubadours' own word, and the
  Preface says so on page one. A chansonnier treatment makes the reader
  hold the tradition Nietzsche claims. Any other library book in this dress
  (a novel, a treatise) would read as costume; here it reads as homecoming.

## Direction C — "VOGELFREI" (outlaw-sea voyage woodcut)

**One-line:** The book as a voyage: Genoa quays, open sea, storm and stars —
  bold German woodcut-poster graphics for the freebooter reader.

- **Palette (dark-first):** Deep Hull Black `#0B0E11` (ground), Signal
  Vermilion `#E8442E`, Chart Cream `#F1E7D3` (linework/text), Sounding Blue
  `#123A5C` (sea), Lantern Amber `#FFB84D`, Storm Acid `#C6F135` (rare
  shock accents: lightning, the heaviest weight).
- **Type:** Archivo (Google Fonts, Expanded 800 for display, a poster
  grotesk with seaworthy stance) + Newsreader (Google Fonts) italic-friendly
  serif for body. Scale: poster-big condensed titles, letterspaced kickers,
  rules like chart graticules.
- **Layout:** cover = a night-harbor poster: huge woodcut wave-scrolls,
  an outlaw ship (black sails) crossing a low amber moon, title as
  broadside masthead; chapters open with a "chart strip": graticule rule +
  sounding-mark motif + position line ("§ 125 · Book Three · Open Sea").
- **Illustration medium:** original SVG woodcut: carved wave-scrolls,
  cross-hatched sea bands, ship/whale/lantern/star-chart blocks in hard
  black/cream with one signal color per part.
- **Signature idea:** the **voyage chart**. The book is a crossing: Preface
  = harbor (Genoa), Prelude = casting off (rigging, gulls), Books 1–2 =
  coastal sailing, Book 3 = storm + dead calm (God dies at sea; "drink up
  the sea"), Book 4 = landfall at dawn (St Januarius sunrise), Book 5 =
  open ocean fearless, Appendix = pirate songs sung on deck. Each part gets
  a chart-panel (latitude lines, sounding numbers, a creature in the margin
  of the unknown). The madman's lantern becomes the ship's lamp.
- **Navigation:** TOC as a chart table with rhumb-lines; progress =
  a voyage track with the ship advancing.
- **Chapter-variation grammar:** sea-state (part) × signal (content):
  chapters carry a marginal sounding-mark (wave/anchor/star/lantern/skull
  for the heaviest weight); landmarks get full chart-panels (Genoa harbor,
  the unchained earth adrift, eternal-recurrence spiral chart).
- **Why THIS book:** "Get on the ships!", Genoa, "Will and wave", the earth
  unchained from its sun adrift like a vessel, Prince Vogelfrei the outlaw
  at sea. The sea-voyage is the book's own master metaphor for thinking
  after God's death. A landlocked treatise could never wear it.

## Recommendation

**Direction A (NOON)** — with B's rubrication discipline for short aphorisms
borrowed as a minor grammar. Reasons: (1) the book's own climaxes are all
solar/diurnal (madman at bright morning, Incipit tragoedia sunrise,
Epilogue's "bright mid-morning"); (2) the day-cycle gives 448 chapters a
single legible progress metaphor no other book can steal; (3) Matisse-ring
dance grammar fits the Prelude's 63 dance-rhymes better than any alternative;
(4) risk (echo of old SUN-DANCE) is manageable: the old theme was a tiny sun
glyph + gradients; this is full-bleed authored mural SVG at architectural
scale. Final choice subject to ArtDirector critique (requested separately).

## ArtDirector critique + choice (2026-10-15, recorded verbatim in essence)

Source: `.omp/design/critiques/gay-science.md` + confirming IRC. Verdict:
**DIRECTION A ("NOON") APPROVED, with binding conditions G1–G6.**

The three questions, answered: (1) Distance from rejected SUN-DANCE is
directionally sufficient but MUST be proven in the render — Prelude rhyme +
§125 side by side; flat wedges, zero radial-gradient glow discs. (2) Prelude
= A with B's discipline, sharpened: 63 rhymes get dance-ring FRAGMENTS
completing the ring across the part; borrow from B exactly one thing —
rubricated first lines in Dance Vermilion; no historiated initials or vines
(two grammars would muddle). (3) Palette/type flags: (a) Noon Gold is the
library's fourth gold — Gay Science owns gold at the LARGEST architectural
scale, everyone else's gold small/hot/sparse/flat; (b) Fraunces is
registry-crowded — Gay Science's must be its wonkiest, most joyful register
(SOFT high, loose leading, dance-tilted kickers); on any confusion move
toward joy, never solemnity.

Binding conditions: G1 day-cycle legible without a legend (8 fixed sun
altitudes; prototype shows three — delivered all eight + six surfaces); G2
scale sensitivity (17-word sparks vs landmark full-bleeds); G3 §125
lantern-at-noon centerpiece; G4 flat sea bands, zero glow discs; G5 Dusk
Aubergine night-only (single exception: §341 hourglass); G6 prototype scope
(cover + Preface + rhyme + §125 + §342 + Book-5 essay + Appendix song at
three viewports — delivered + §275 seal proof).

Shared principles P1–P10 (`.omp/design/ART-DIRECTION.md`) bind the build:
authored image at scale, title-swap test, verified webfonts, authored
per-content chapter art, calm reading body, per-book composition,
dark-first/all-themes, motion-answers-action, evidence over claims.

**Choice: Direction A (NOON).** B rejected: beautiful but static manuscript
craft for a book that dances. C rejected: voyage grammar would drown 168
tiny Book-3 aphorisms in chart furniture. A's day-cycle sun is the only
signature spanning mischievous rhymes AND the §125 abyss AND amor-fati
sunrise without splitting in two. Binding spec:
`books/gay-science/design/ART-DIRECTION.md`.
