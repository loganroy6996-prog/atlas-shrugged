### Proposal — p1c5
- **Primary motif: "UTI POSSIDETIS — the surveyor's chart."** An engraved-map opener: fine copperplate linework showing a coastline with **demarcation boundary lines** (the 1967 lines as literal drawn lines, dashed "fuzzy jurisdiction" around a West-Bank-like enclave), rhumb lines, a small Latin cartouche reading *UTI POSSIDETIS*, and a mesh of small equal sovereign nodes (peer-to-peer) contrasted with one faint hub-and-spoke (client-server) tucked in a corner as the rejected alternative. Medium: original SVG linework in the manner of a baroque map cartouche — hatching, stippled sea, serif legend. This is the chapter's thesis made visible: peace = drawn lines respected [241–246]. Nothing else in the cohort is about maps, borders, or international law.
- **Fallback motif: "'Other grain.'"** A split sheaf: wheat ears whose grains, on close inspection, are powder-corns (black grains among gold), after Franklin's anecdote [219–221]. Smaller, more emblematic; works if the chart proves too busy at small sizes.
- **Opener composition (1440px):** asymmetric editorial spread — the chart occupies the left ~55% as a tall engraved plate with its cartouche; the title block sits right, top-aligned with the cartouche, with the Stross dedication ("For Charles Stross") set as an inscription line. The demarcation lines bleed off the plate's right edge toward the title, visually "fixing" the border between art and text.
- **Collapse (390px):** chart stacks above title at ~200px height, cropped to the cartouche + one boundary line; legend text hidden except *UTI POSSIDETIS*; no horizontal scroll; linework stays ≥1px.
- **Palette role (extends oxblood/copper, does not clone):** *Surveyor's brass* `#C9A86A` (linework/legend), *Chart-sea ink* `#16323E` (plate ground, deep teal-black), *Demarcation red* `#B3402E` (boundary lines — a drier, more vermilion red than book accent `#c8614f`), parchment highlight `#E8D9B5` (cartouche text). The teal-sea ground is the chapter's distinctive note — nowhere else in the book.
- **Reading treatment:** (a) **Epistolary pull treatment for the Stross letter**: opening paras [0–4] set with a hanging "My dear Charlie" inscription style — slightly indented block, brass left tick (a drawn line, not a border), small-caps dateline feel; (b) **Section-break mark:** a surveyor's boundary cairn `∴` flanked by short dashed rule segments (evokes demarcation lines); (c) **Document treatment:** the Federal Council `•` list [176–185] and long quoted excerpts (Davis [234–237], Tumulty [127–139]) get an "exhibit plate" style — numbered exhibits (Exhibit A/B/C…), chart-sea wash background, legend-caption styling — because the chapter's method is literally *presenting documents*; (d) Drop cap: yes, but a cartouche-framed initial (initial inside a small engraved frame) rather than the book-default wash.
- **Why it cannot be swapped:** p1c6 is about the *internal machinery* of one government (corporation, keys, crypto) and p1c7 about *hidden domestic power* (masks, camouflage) — neither contains a single map, border, treaty, or foreign capital. The surveyor's chart belongs to the only chapter whose plan is drawn in lines on the earth [241–248].

### Proposal — p1c6
- **Primary motif: "The keychain of command."** A CDCC emblem: an engraved **skeleton key whose bit is a circuit trace**, duplicated as a chain of three interlocking keys labeled (in engraved micro-caps) SECURE · EFFECTIVE · RESPONSIBLE — the chapter's triad [26] rendered as literal cryptographic hardware [78–85]. Behind/around: faint schematic lines connecting a small crowned administrator's seal (front-end), a single enthroned figure-silhouette (Steve/middle), and a ring of shareholder-seals (back-end/controllers [40–41]). Medium: original SVG combining copperplate engraving strokes with circuit-board geometry — "brushed aluminum meets the strongroom." This is the only chapter about *mechanism design*, and the key literalizes both sovereignty's oldest symbol and cryptography's newest.
- **Fallback motif: "Calmeat vs. the King."** A deadpan diptych emblem: a sorry state-issued burger token vs. a crown — the subjective test [19–22]. More playful, less dignified; better suited to a section mark than the opener.
- **Opener composition (1440px):** centered strongroom-door composition — the three-key emblem large (~320px) above the title like a seal on a charter, with the schematic lines spreading horizontally behind the title block; the definition sentence [15] set as an engraved charter line beneath the title. Symmetric, frontal, engineered — the visual opposite of p1c5's asymmetric chart.
- **Collapse (390px):** single key (the middle "EFFECTIVE" key) at ~120px above title; triad labels stack as three short lines under the title; schematic traces hidden.
- **Palette role:** *Brushed aluminum* `#B9C2C9` (key metal, linework — the chapter's signature material, from [66]), *Strongroom brass* `#E0B34C` (key highlights, triad labels; brighter and more golden than book copper), *Verdigris circuit* `#3F7D6B` (schematic traces — a cold green counterpoint unique in the book), *Charter ink* `#0E1418` (opener ground, near-black blue). Aluminum + verdigris on dark blue-black reads "machine room," unmistakable against p1c5's teal-sea map and the book's oxblood.
- **Reading treatment:** (a) **Triad marginal device:** each section's margin carries the S/E/R key-mark for whichever leg is under discussion (effectiveness [26–39], responsibility [40–72], security [73–86]) — a genuine navigational aid for the chapter's architecture; (b) **Definition charter:** para [15] ("A government is a sovereign corporation") and the triad sentence [26] set as charter lines — centered, letterspaced, key-flanked; (c) **Section-break mark:** a tiny key-bit glyph `⚿`-like (authored, not emoji); (d) Drop cap: no — the engineer's chapter opens clean; instead the opener seal carries the weight. The Calmeat [21–22] and battery-rant [83–84] passages get a dry "exhibit" aside style shared with p1c5's exhibits but in aluminum rather than chart-sea.
- **Why it cannot be swapped:** p1c5 never designs anything (it *recovers* old law) and p1c7 never builds anything (it *diagnoses* present power). Only p1c6 contains corporations, CEOs, keys, circuits, aluminum, dividends, or the S/E/R triad. A cryptographic keychain would be meaningless atop the Quaker history or the Cathedral diagnosis.
